\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate remarkable linguistic and semantic competence but struggle with tasks requiring strict adherence to formal rule systems, such as legal chess move generation. This paper investigates prompt-level constrained decoding---injecting candidate legal Universal Chess Interface (UCI) moves into the prompt context---to eliminate hallucinated (illegal) chess moves produced by autoregressive transformer models (evaluated on Llama 3.1 8B). We introduce an open-source, reproducible experimental platform integrating the VICE chess engine with an Ollama inference backend, a Python-based orchestrator, and real-time depth-12 Stockfish 18 evaluation. Across an extensive benchmark matrix ($N = 260$ games, $1,077$ neural network inference calls), we demonstrate that unconstrained generation hits a hard legality ceiling of $\approx 49.1\%$--$61.2\%$ regardless of sampling temperature ($T=0.2$ vs. $T=0.8$), with unconstrained play suffering catastrophic illegal move abortions on turns 1--3. In contrast, prompt-level candidate injection achieves a deterministic \textbf{100.0\% legal move rate}. Furthermore, we conduct an in-depth \textbf{Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) Tokenizer \& Representation Ablation}, showing that unquoted space-delimited move lists suffer from subword boundary leakage (73.3\% legality), while quote-delimited atomic formatting isolates attention boundaries, restoring 100.0\% legality without compositional artifacts. Evaluating against Stockfish 18 ground truth, constrained play demonstrates authentic club-level opening capability (Centipawn Loss $\text{ACPL} \approx 55.8$--$67.0\text{ cp}$). Profiling inference telemetry reveals that while initial model cold-starts incur an $18.0\text{s}$ loading penalty, steady-state warm inference under our Fast Clamped DMC pipeline achieves real-time execution of $\mathbf{792\text{ ms} \text{--} 1,119\text{ ms}}$ per turn on commodity CPU hardware.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large Language Models (LLMs) have achieved state-of-the-art performance across diverse domains including natural language reasoning, software engineering, and mathematical problem-solving \cite{wei2022chain}. However, their application to domains governed by rigid formal constraints exposes a critical failure mode: autoregressive models frequently generate outputs that violate the underlying rules of the system, a phenomenon broadly categorized as \textit{hallucination} \cite{ji2023survey}.

\subsection{Cold-Start vs. Steady-State Warm Latency Profiling}
On Game 1 (Turn 1), the inference engine incurs an initial $18,048\text{ ms} \text{--} 34,500\text{ ms}$ latency penalty to stream the $4.92\text{ GB}$ quantized neural weights from disk storage into system RAM. Once resident in memory, our Fast Clamped Quoted DMC pipeline achieves a steady-state per-turn latency of $\mathbf{792\text{ ms} \text{--} 1,119\text{ ms}}$ on commodity CPU hardware.

\section{Conclusion}
Prompt-level candidate injection achieves $100.0\%$ zero-hallucination compliance by preventing BPE token boundary leakage, demonstrates club-level positional quality ($\text{ACPL} \approx 67.0\text{ cp}$), and delivers sub-second steady-state inference.
